A Warm Welcome to You
Hey there! First off, welcome to the team. We are genuinely excited
to have you here. This handbook is not some boring legal document
meant to confuse you. Think of it as your friendly guide to
understanding how things work around here.
We wrote this in plain, simple English because we believe policies
should be easy to understand. If you ever read something here and
think, "Wait, what does that actually mean?" please reach out to
the HR team. We are here to help, not to judge.
This document covers everything from your working hours and leave
policies to how we handle performance reviews, workplace safety,
and what to do if something is bothering you. Read it at your own
pace. You do not need to memorize it. You can always come back to
it whenever you have a question.
So grab a coffee, get comfortable, and let us walk you through
everything you need to know.

SECTION 1: EMPLOYMENT BASICS
1.1 Employment Types
We have a few different types of employment here, and it is
important to know which one applies to you.
Full-Time Employees: These are folks who work a standard 40-hour
week, usually Monday through Friday. Full-time employees get the
full package of benefits including health insurance, paid leave,
and retirement plans.
Part-Time Employees: If you work fewer than 30 hours per week,
you are considered part-time. You still get access to many
benefits, though some may be prorated based on your hours.
Contract Workers: Sometimes we bring people on for a specific
project or a fixed period. Contract workers are hired for a set
duration, and the terms are clearly spelled out in your contract.
Interns: We love our interns! Internships can be paid or unpaid,
and they usually last between three to six months. Interns get
mentorship, learning opportunities, and in many cases, a pathway
to full-time roles.
1.2 Probation Period
When you first join us, you will go through a probation period.
This is usually 90 days, though for some senior roles it might
stretch to six months.
Think of probation as a mutual getting-to-know-each-other phase.
We get to see if the role is a good fit for you, and you get to
decide if we are the right place for you. During probation, you
still get your salary and basic benefits. If things are not
working out during this period, either side can part ways with
a two-week notice.
After probation, you become a confirmed employee, and your notice
period and benefits kick in fully.
1.3 Your Employee ID and Records
On your first day, HR will assign you an employee ID number.
Keep it handy because you will use it for everything from logging
into systems to requesting leave.
We also keep basic records about you. Your name, contact details,
emergency contacts, tax information, and banking details for
salary deposits. If any of this changes, like you move to a new
address or change your phone number, please update HR within
seven days. It helps us keep everything running smoothly.

SECTION 2: WORKING HOURS AND ATTENDANCE
2.1 Standard Working Hours
Our standard working hours are 9:00 AM to 5:30 PM, Monday through
Friday, with a 30-minute lunch break. That gives you roughly 8
hours of work per day and 40 hours per week.
Now, we understand that life does not always fit neatly into a
9-to-5 box. If you need to shift your hours a little, like
starting at 8 AM and leaving at 4:30 PM, just talk to your
manager. As long as the work gets done and it does not affect
team collaboration, we are flexible.
2.2 Flexible Working Hours
We trust our people. That is why we offer flexible working hours.
The core hours when everyone should be available for meetings and
collaboration are 10:00 AM to 3:00 PM. Outside of that window,
you can adjust your start and end times.
For example, if you are a morning person, you might start at
7:30 AM and wrap up by 4:00 PM. If you need to drop kids at
school first, maybe you start at 9:30 AM and stay a little later.
The key is communication. Let your team know your schedule so
nobody is left waiting.
2.3 Attendance and Punctuality
We get it. Traffic happens. Alarms do not go off. Kids get sick.
We are human, and we understand that.
That said, we do ask that you try your best to be on time. If
you know you are going to be late, just send a quick message to
your manager or team. A simple "Hey, running 15 minutes late
because of traffic" goes a long way.
If you are going to be absent, please let your manager know as
early as possible, ideally before your shift starts. For planned
absences, give at least 24 hours notice.
2.4 Overtime
We do not encourage overtime. We want you to have a life outside
of work. However, sometimes deadlines demand extra effort.
If you need to work more than 40 hours in a week, it must be
approved by your manager in advance. Overtime is compensated at
1.5 times your regular hourly rate, or you can choose to take
compensatory time off instead. You get to pick whichever works
better for you.
No one should feel pressured to work overtime. If you find
yourself regularly working late, please talk to your manager.
We might need to look at workload distribution or deadlines.
2.5 Remote and Hybrid Work
We offer a hybrid work model. You can work from home up to three
days a week, and the remaining two days are in the office. The
specific days can be arranged with your team to make sure there
is always someone in the office for collaboration.
For fully remote roles, all the same policies apply. You just
do it from your home office instead of ours.
When working remotely, we ask that you maintain a quiet,
professional workspace for calls and meetings. Make sure your
internet connection is stable. And yes, wearing pajama pants
during a video call is technically allowed, but we might gently
tease you about it.

SECTION 3: LEAVE POLICIES
3.1 Annual Leave (Vacation)
Everyone needs a break, and we want you to take yours.
Full-time employees get 20 days of paid annual leave per year.
That is four full weeks to relax, travel, spend time with family,
or just binge-watch your favorite shows without guilt.
Annual leave accrues monthly. You earn about 1.67 days per month.
You can carry over up to 5 unused days to the next year, but
anything beyond that will lapse. We genuinely encourage you to
use your leave. Burnout is real, and rest is not a luxury.
To request annual leave, submit your request through the HR
portal at least two weeks in advance for planned vacations.
For short trips of one or two days, a week's notice is fine.
3.2 Sick Leave
If you are feeling under the weather, stay home. Seriously.
Do not come in and spread germs. We have all been there, and
nobody wants to be the person who gets the whole office sick.
You get 10 days of paid sick leave per year. For one or two
days of sick leave, you do not need a doctor's note. Just
inform your manager by the morning. If you are sick for three
or more consecutive days, we will need a medical certificate
from your doctor.
If you exhaust your sick leave and are still unwell, we will
work with you to arrange unpaid leave or use any remaining
annual leave. Your health comes first, always.
3.3 Personal Leave
Sometimes life just happens. A plumbing emergency, a family
matter, a court appearance, or you just need a mental health
day. We get it.
You get 5 days of personal leave per year. These are paid days,
and you can use them for any reason. You do not need to explain
or justify why. Just give your manager a heads-up as early as
possible.
3.4 Public Holidays
We observe all national and local public holidays. These are
paid days off, and you do not need to use your annual leave
for them. A list of holidays for the year is shared every
January through the HR portal and on the office notice board.
If a public holiday falls on a weekend, we usually observe it
on the following Monday, but this will be communicated in
advance.
3.5 Parental Leave
We are proud to support parents. Whether you are welcoming a
new baby through birth, adoption, or surrogacy, we have got
you covered.
Maternity Leave: New mothers get 16 weeks of fully paid
maternity leave. You can start your leave up to two weeks
before your due date. If you need additional time for medical
reasons, we will extend it.
Paternity Leave: New fathers or partners get 4 weeks of fully
paid paternity leave. Use it to bond with your little one and
support your partner.
Adoption Leave: If you are adopting a child, you get 12 weeks
of paid leave, regardless of gender.
Parental leave is a right, not a favor. Taking it will not
affect your career progression, performance reviews, or
standing in the company. Period.
3.6 Bereavement Leave
We are deeply sorry if you ever need to use this leave. In the
event of losing an immediate family member (spouse, child,
parent, sibling), you get 5 days of paid bereavement leave.
For extended family members like grandparents, aunts, uncles,
or in-laws, you get 3 days.
Take the time you need to grieve and be with your family.
Work will wait. If you need additional time beyond what is
provided, talk to HR and we will figure something out together.
3.7 Jury Duty and Civic Leave
If you are called for jury duty or need to vote, you get paid
time off. We respect civic responsibilities. Just bring your
jury summons or relevant documentation to HR, and we will
handle the rest.

SECTION 4: COMPENSATION AND BENEFITS
4.1 Salary and Pay Schedule
You will be paid on the last business day of every month via
direct deposit to your bank account. Your salary details are
outlined in your offer letter and employment contract.
If there is ever a discrepancy in your pay, please reach out
to the payroll team within five business days. Mistakes happen,
and we will fix them quickly.
4.2 Annual Salary Review
We conduct salary reviews once a year, usually in April.
Your manager and HR will look at your performance, market
rates, and the company's financial health to determine any
adjustments.
A raise is not guaranteed every year, but we are committed to
fair and competitive compensation. If you feel your pay does
not reflect your contributions, you are welcome to have an
open conversation with your manager or HR.
4.3 Bonuses
Depending on company performance and individual contributions,
we may offer annual bonuses. These are typically announced in
the first quarter of the following year. Bonuses are not
guaranteed and are at the company's discretion, but we love
rewarding great work.
4.4 Health Insurance
We provide comprehensive health insurance for all full-time
employees. This includes medical, dental, and vision coverage.
You can also add your spouse and dependent children to your
plan at a subsidized rate.
Your health insurance kicks in from your first day of
employment. There is no waiting period. The details of your
coverage, network hospitals, and claim procedures are in the
benefits package you received during onboarding.
4.5 Retirement and Savings Plan
We offer a retirement savings plan. The company matches your
contributions up to 6 percent of your base salary. This is
essentially free money for your future, so we highly encourage
you to enroll.
You can adjust your contribution percentage at any time
through the HR portal.
4.6 Other Benefits
Here are some additional perks that we offer:
Gym membership reimbursement up to a set monthly amount.
Annual learning and development budget for courses, books,
or conferences.
Employee assistance program for mental health support and
counseling. This is completely confidential.
Free snacks and beverages in the office. Yes, the good
coffee is included.
Birthday half-day off. Your birthday is your day. Enjoy it.
Quarterly team outings and an annual company retreat.

SECTION 5: PERFORMANCE AND GROWTH
5.1 Performance Reviews
We do performance reviews twice a year, once in June and once
in December. These are not scary interrogations. Think of them
as structured conversations about how you are doing, what is
going well, and where you can grow.
Your manager will schedule a one-on-one meeting with you.
Before the meeting, you will fill out a self-assessment form
where you reflect on your achievements, challenges, and goals.
Your manager will do the same. Then you sit down together and
talk it through.
The goal is not to criticize. The goal is to help you grow
and make sure you feel supported.
5.2 Feedback Culture
We believe in continuous feedback, not just waiting for
bi-annual reviews. If your manager does something great,
tell them. If you need help or something is not working,
speak up.
We encourage a culture of open, honest, and kind feedback.
Constructive feedback is a gift, even if it stings a little
at first.
5.3 Promotions and Career Growth
We love promoting from within. If you are doing great work
and showing leadership, we want to help you move up.
Promotions are based on performance, skill development, and
business needs. Talk to your manager about your career goals.
They can help you identify what you need to work on and
connect you with opportunities.
5.4 Training and Development
Every employee gets an annual learning budget. You can use it
for online courses, workshops, certifications, books, or
industry conferences.
We also run internal training sessions every quarter on topics
like leadership, communication, technical skills, and wellness.
These are optional but highly encouraged.
If you want to pursue a certification or degree that is
relevant to your role, talk to HR. We may be able to sponsor
part or all of the cost.

SECTION 6: CODE OF CONDUCT
6.1 Our Values
Everything we do is guided by a few core values:
Respect: We treat everyone with dignity. No exceptions.
Whether you are talking to the CEO or the intern, the
receptionist or the cleaning staff, everyone deserves respect.
Integrity: We do the right thing, even when nobody is watching.
We are honest in our work, our communications, and our
relationships.
Accountability: We own our mistakes. We do not blame others
or sweep things under the rug. We learn and we grow.
Collaboration: We are a team. We help each other, share
knowledge, and celebrate each other's wins.
Inclusivity: We welcome diversity in all forms. Different
backgrounds, perspectives, and experiences make us stronger.
6.2 Professional Behavior
We expect everyone to behave professionally at all times,
whether in the office, at a client site, at a company event,
or in work-related online communications.
This means no yelling, no name-calling, no throwing things,
and no passive-aggressive emails that make everyone
uncomfortable. If you are frustrated, take a breath, step
away, and come back when you are calm.
6.3 Dress Code
Our dress code is business casual. What does that mean?
It means look neat and presentable, but you do not need a
three-piece suit every day.
Jeans are fine on Fridays. Sneakers are okay. Just maybe
skip the beach shorts and flip-flops unless it is a themed
day.
If you are meeting clients, dress one level up. When in
doubt, ask your manager.
6.4 Substance Policy
We have a zero-tolerance policy for being under the influence
of alcohol or illegal drugs during working hours. This is
about safety, not judgment.
If you are on prescription medication that might affect your
ability to work safely, please let your manager or HR know
confidentially so we can make accommodations.
We do host company events where alcohol may be served. At
these events, drink responsibly. You are still representing
the company, and the same conduct rules apply.
6.5 Conflict of Interest
If you have a personal relationship with a colleague, a
client, or a vendor that could influence your work decisions,
you need to disclose it to HR. This is not about policing
your personal life. It is about transparency and fairness.
For example, if your spouse works for a vendor that your
team is evaluating, you should recuse yourself from that
decision. Simple as that.

SECTION 7: ANTI-HARASSMENT AND ANTI-DISCRIMINATION
7.1 Our Stance
Let us be absolutely clear. We do not tolerate harassment or
discrimination of any kind. Not a little bit. Not as a joke.
Not "just banter." Zero tolerance.
This applies to everyone: employees, managers, contractors,
clients, vendors, visitors. Everyone.
7.2 What Counts as Harassment
Harassment is any unwelcome behavior that makes someone feel
unsafe, degraded, or uncomfortable. This includes but is not
limited to:
Unwanted physical contact or touching.
Sexual comments, jokes, or gestures.
Making inappropriate comments about someone's body,
appearance, or personal life.
Sending unwanted messages, emails, or images of a sexual
or offensive nature.
Bullying, intimidation, or threats.
Excluding someone deliberately because of who they are.
Repeatedly targeting someone with negative comments or
criticism that goes beyond normal work feedback.
7.3 What Counts as Discrimination
Discrimination is treating someone unfairly because of their
race, gender, age, religion, disability, sexual orientation,
nationality, marital status, or any other protected
characteristic.
This includes hiring decisions, promotions, pay, work
assignments, and day-to-day interactions. Everyone gets a
fair shot and equal treatment. Period.
7.4 How to Report
If you experience or witness harassment or discrimination,
please report it. You can:
Talk to your direct manager (unless they are the problem).
Contact HR directly via email or in person.
Use the anonymous reporting tool on our intranet.
Reach out to any member of the leadership team.
You do not have to figure it out alone. You do not have to
have "proof" to come forward. Just tell us what happened,
and we will investigate.
7.5 What Happens After You Report
We take every report seriously. Here is the process:
Step 1: We acknowledge your report within 24 hours.
Step 2: HR assigns an investigator. This person is neutral
and trained in handling these matters.
Step 3: We gather information, speak to relevant parties,
and review any evidence.
Step 4: We make a determination and take appropriate action.
Step 5: We follow up with you to make sure you feel safe
and supported.
The entire process is confidential to the extent possible.
We will not share details with people who do not need to know.
7.6 No Retaliation
If you report something, you will not be punished for it.
You will not be demoted, sidelined, or treated differently.
Retaliation against someone who reports harassment or
discrimination is itself a serious offense and will be
dealt with firmly.

SECTION 8: HEALTH AND SAFETY
8.1 A Safe Workplace
Your safety is our top priority. We maintain a clean, safe,
and healthy work environment. All office equipment, furniture,
and facilities are regularly inspected and maintained.
If you notice a safety hazard, like a loose wire, a wet floor,
a broken chair, or a blocked fire exit, report it immediately
to facilities management or your manager. Do not assume
someone else will do it.
8.2 Ergonomics
Sitting at a desk all day can take a toll on your body. We
provide ergonomic chairs, adjustable desks, and monitor
stands. If you need specific equipment to work comfortably,
like a special keyboard or a standing desk, just ask.
We encourage you to take short breaks every hour. Stand up,
stretch, walk around, get some water. Your body will thank you.
8.3 Mental Health
Mental health is health. We recognize that stress, anxiety,
depression, and other mental health challenges are real and
valid.
Through our Employee Assistance Program (EAP), you can access
free, confidential counseling sessions. No one at the company
will know you used it unless you choose to share.
If you are going through a tough time and need a lighter
workload temporarily, talk to your manager or HR. We will
work with you.
8.4 Emergency Procedures
Every floor has clearly marked emergency exits, fire
extinguishers, and first aid kits. Fire drills are conducted
twice a year. Please participate. They are quick, and they
could save your life.
In case of a medical emergency, call the front desk or
dial the emergency number posted near the elevators. We have
trained first aid responders on every floor.
If there is a fire, do not use the elevator. Use the stairs.
Leave your belongings. Just get out.

SECTION 9: TECHNOLOGY AND DATA SECURITY
9.1 Company Devices
If the company provides you with a laptop, phone, or any
other device, please take care of it. These are tools for
work, and we ask that you treat them responsibly.
Do not install unauthorized software. Do not lend your
device to others. Keep your passwords secure. If your device
is lost or stolen, report it to IT immediately so we can
remotely wipe it and protect company data.
When you leave the company, you will need to return all
devices and accessories.
9.2 Internet and Email Use
You can use the company internet and email for reasonable
personal use. Checking your personal email during lunch or
looking up a recipe is totally fine.
What is not fine is using company resources to access
illegal content, download pirated software, visit inappropriate
websites, or conduct a side business during work hours.
9.3 Passwords and Security
Use strong, unique passwords for all work accounts. Do not
write them on sticky notes and put them on your monitor.
We know it is tempting, but please resist.
Enable two-factor authentication wherever possible. If you
get a suspicious email or link, do not click it. Report it
to the IT security team. Phishing attacks are real, and
they are getting sneakier.
9.4 Data Protection and Privacy
We handle a lot of sensitive data, including employee
information, client data, and financial records. You are
expected to keep this information confidential.
Do not share company data on personal email, personal cloud
storage, or unsecured devices. Do not discuss sensitive
client information in public places like cafes or on public
transport.
If you are unsure whether something is confidential, assume
it is and ask your manager.

SECTION 10: CONFIDENTIALITY AND INTELLECTUAL PROPERTY
10.1 Confidential Information
During your time here, you will have access to information
that is not public. This includes business strategies,
financial data, client lists, product plans, employee
records, and internal communications.
You agree to keep this information confidential during and
after your employment. This is not just a policy. In many
cases, it is also a legal obligation outlined in your
employment contract.
10.2 Intellectual Property
Anything you create as part of your job, whether it is
code, designs, documents, presentations, ideas, or
inventions, belongs to the company. This is standard
practice and is outlined in your contract.
If you have a personal project or idea that you developed
outside of work and on your own time, that is yours. Just
make sure it does not conflict with your work here or use
any company resources.
10.3 Non-Disclosure
You may be asked to sign non-disclosure agreements for
specific projects or client work. Please read them carefully.
If you have questions, ask HR or legal before signing.

SECTION 11: DISCIPLINARY PROCEDURES
11.1 Our Approach
We do not jump to punishment. Our approach is corrective,
not punitive. If something goes wrong, we want to understand
why and help you do better.
That said, serious misconduct will be dealt with firmly.
11.2 Steps of Disciplinary Action
For minor issues like repeated tardiness, missing deadlines
without communication, or minor policy violations, here is
the process:
Step 1 - Verbal Warning: Your manager will have a private
conversation with you. This is not a formal reprimand. It
is a heads-up. "Hey, this needs to improve." It is noted
informally.
Step 2 - Written Warning: If the behavior continues, you
will receive a formal written warning. This goes into your
HR file. It will clearly state what the issue is and what
needs to change.
Step 3 - Final Written Warning: If there is still no
improvement, you get a final written warning. This is
serious. It will outline the specific consequences if the
behavior does not change.
Step 4 - Termination: If the issue persists after all
warnings, we may have to end the employment. This is a
last resort, and we genuinely hope it never gets here.
11.3 Serious Misconduct
Some things skip the warning process entirely. These include:
Theft, fraud, or deliberate damage to company property.
Violence or threats of violence.
Harassment or discrimination.
Being under the influence of drugs or alcohol at work.
Serious breach of confidentiality.
Falsifying company records or documents.
In these cases, we may suspend you immediately while we
investigate, and termination may follow.
11.4 Your Right to Respond
At every stage of the disciplinary process, you have the
right to tell your side of the story. You can bring a
colleague or a representative to any disciplinary meeting.
You will not be penalized for speaking up.

SECTION 12: GRIEVANCE PROCEDURES
12.1 If Something is Bothering You
If you have a concern, a complaint, or a disagreement about
your work, your manager, your colleagues, or any company
policy, please do not suffer in silence. We want to know,
and we want to help.
12.2 How to Raise a Grievance
Start by talking to your direct manager. Many issues can be
resolved with a simple, honest conversation.
If you are not comfortable talking to your manager, or if
your manager is part of the problem, go to HR. You can send
an email, walk into the HR office, or use the anonymous
grievance form on the intranet.
12.3 What Happens Next
Once we receive your grievance, here is what we do:
We acknowledge it within two business days.
We assign someone to look into it.
We gather information from all relevant parties.
We try to find a fair resolution.
We communicate the outcome to you.
The whole process should take no more than 10 to 15 business
days, depending on the complexity.
12.4 Escalation
If you are not satisfied with the outcome, you can escalate
the grievance to the Head of HR or the CEO. You can also
seek external mediation or legal advice. We will not hold
that against you.

SECTION 13: TERMINATION AND RESIGNATION
13.1 Resignation
If you decide to move on, we are sad to see you go, but we
respect your decision.
During probation, the notice period is two weeks. After
probation, the standard notice period is 30 days. For senior
roles, it may be 60 or 90 days as specified in your contract.
Please submit your resignation in writing, either via email
to your manager and HR or through the HR portal.
13.2 Exit Process
Once you resign, HR will schedule an exit interview. This
is a casual, honest conversation about your experience here.
What did you like? What could we do better? Your feedback
helps us improve.
You will need to return all company property, complete any
pending handovers, and clear any outstanding dues. HR will
guide you through the checklist.
Your final salary, any unused leave encashment, and other
dues will be processed within 30 days of your last working day.
13.3 Termination by the Company
We do not take termination lightly. It can happen due to
performance issues (after the disciplinary process outlined
in Section 11), redundancy, restructuring, or serious
misconduct.
If your role is made redundant, you will receive a notice
period and severance pay as per your contract and local
labor laws. We will also do our best to help you with
references and job search support.
13.4 References
If you need a reference after leaving, HR will provide one
based on your employment records. We keep it factual and fair.

We support employee resource groups (ERGs) for various
communities within the company. These are voluntary,
employee-led groups that provide support, networking, and
advocacy. If you want to start one, talk to HR. We will
help you get it going.

The company provides travel insurance for all business trips.

and lost luggage. Details are available from HR before you travel.

on your own time.

If a journalist, blogger, or anyone from the media contacts

16.3 Company Social Media
We encourage you to engage with the company's social media

positive.

SECTION 17: WORKPLACE RELATIONSHIPS
17.1 Romantic Relationships
We are adults, and we understand that relationships can form
at work. We do not ban workplace romances. However, we do
ask for transparency.
If you are in a romantic relationship with a colleague,
please inform HR, especially if one of you reports to the
other. This is not to judge. It is to manage potential
conflicts of interest and ensure fairness in performance
reviews, promotions, and project assignments.
If the relationship ends, both parties are expected to
maintain professionalism at work.
17.2 Friendships and Team Dynamics
We encourage friendships at work. They make the day better.
But we also ask that you be inclusive. Do not form cliques
that exclude others. Be welcoming to new team members.
Say hi to people in the hallway. Small things matter.

SECTION 18: FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS
Q: How many days of annual leave do I get?
A: Full-time employees get 20 days of paid annual leave per
year, accrued monthly at about 1.67 days per month.
Q: Can I carry over unused leave?
A: Yes, you can carry over up to 5 days to the next year.
Anything beyond that lapses.
Q: How many sick days do I get?
A: You get 10 days of paid sick leave per year. You do not
need a doctor's note for one or two days.
Q: What is the probation period?
A: The standard probation period is 90 days. For some senior
roles, it may be up to six months.
Q: When do I get paid?
A: You get paid on the last business day of every month via
direct deposit.
Q: Can I work from home?
A: Yes. We follow a hybrid model. You can work from home up
to three days a week. Fully remote roles are also available
for certain positions.
Q: What is the dress code?
A: Business casual. Jeans on Fridays. Keep it neat and
presentable.
Q: How do I report harassment?
A: You can report it to your manager, HR directly, or through
the anonymous reporting tool on the intranet. Every report
is taken seriously and investigated confidentially.
Q: What if I need a mental health day?
A: You can use your personal leave days for mental health.
You do not need to explain or justify. Just let your manager
know you will be out.
Q: How do I request a leave?
A: Submit your request through the HR portal. For annual
leave, give at least two weeks notice for planned vacations.
For sick or emergency leave, inform your manager as early
as possible.
Q: What happens if I am late to work?
A: Occasional lateness is understood. Just communicate with
your manager. Repeated tardiness without communication may
lead to a verbal warning as part of the disciplinary process.
Q: Can I use my personal phone during work hours?
A: Yes, for reasonable personal use. Just make sure it does
not interfere with your work. During meetings, please keep
it on silent.
Q: What benefits do I get as a full-time employee?
A: Health insurance (medical, dental, vision), retirement
plan with company match, 20 days annual leave, 10 days sick
leave, 5 days personal leave, learning budget, gym
reimbursement, and more.
Q: How do performance reviews work?
A: We do them twice a year, in June and December. You fill
out a self-assessment, your manager does the same, and then
you have a one-on-one conversation about growth and goals.
Q: What is the notice period for resignation?
A: During probation, it is two weeks. After probation, it is
30 days for most roles and up to 90 days for senior positions.
Q: Can I get a reference after I leave?
A: Yes. HR will provide a factual and fair reference based
on your employment records.
Q: What if I have a grievance against my manager?
A: You can go directly to HR or use the anonymous grievance
form. You do not have to go through your manager if they are
the issue.
Q: Is overtime paid?
A: Yes, if it is pre-approved by your manager. Overtime is
compensated at 1.5 times your regular rate, or you can take
compensatory time off.
Q: What is the company's policy on remote work equipment?
A: For fully remote employees, the company provides a laptop
and a stipend for setting up a home office. For hybrid
employees, you use the office equipment when in the office
and can take your laptop home on remote days.
Q: How does the Employee Assistance Program work?
A: You can access free, confidential counseling sessions
through our EAP provider. Details are in your benefits
package. No one at the company will know you used it.
